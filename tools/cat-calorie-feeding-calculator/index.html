How to Balance Wet and Dry Food for Your Cat

  1. 1

    Enter your cat's ideal weight and life stage

    Select Indoor Neutered (1.2×), Weight Loss (0.8×), Active Outdoor (1.6×), or Kitten (2.5×).

  2. 2

    Enter daily wet food cans and kcal per can

    A standard 3 oz (85 g) cat food can averages 75–95 kcal; enter 0 for dry-only diets.

  3. 3

    Enter your dry kibble's calorie density

    Check the bag for kcal/cup (avg 400) and kcal/kg (avg 3,800).

  4. 4

    Measure the remaining dry kibble in grams

    Weighing dry food on a gram scale prevents indoor cat obesity far better than free-feeding scoops.

Exact Formula & Worked Example

Cat RER (kcal)     = 70 * (weight_kg ^ 0.75)
Cat DER (kcal)     = RER * feline_stage_factor
Remaining Dry kcal = max(0, DER - (wet_cans * kcal_per_can))
Dry Kibble (g/day) = (Remaining Dry kcal / dry_kcal_per_kg) * 1000

Worked Example: A 10 lb (4.54 kg) indoor neutered cat has RER = 218 kcal and DER = 262 kcal/day (1.2×). After feeding one 3 oz wet can (85 kcal), 177 kcal remain for dry kibble—equal to exactly 47 g (0.44 cup) of 3,800 kcal/kg dry food per day.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories does a 10 lb indoor cat need per day?

A healthy 10 lb (4.5 kg) neutered indoor cat has an RER of 218 kcal and needs about 262 kcal/day (1.2x RER). If fed one 3 oz wet can (85 kcal), they need 177 kcal of dry kibble (~47 g or 0.44 cup) per day.

How do I mix wet and dry food for my cat without overfeeding?

First calculate your cat's total daily calorie need (DER), subtract the exact calories from all wet food cans/pouches fed each day, and measure only the remaining calories in dry kibble using a digital gram scale.

How many calories should an overweight cat eat to lose weight?

Veterinarians typically start indoor cat weight loss at 0.8x RER based on ideal target weight (usually 170–210 kcal/day for a 10 lb ideal cat). Never fast a cat—rapid starvation risks hepatic lipidosis.
